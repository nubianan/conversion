Sample rate, bit depth, bitrate · three things people mix up, explained simply and then shown
1
Sample rate is the frame rate of sound
How many snapshots of the sound are taken every second. "44.1 kHz" just means 44,100 snapshots per second, the same way a camera shoots 24 frames per second. It's a counting speed, not a sound you hear. It decides how high a pitch can be captured.
The camera version · a bouncing ball, filmed frame by frame
The sound version · one pitch, measured as dots
SnapshotsPitch
2
Bit depth is the number of marks on the amplitude ruler
How precisely each snapshot's amplitude (the height of the wave) is measured. The loudest possible level is the same at any bit depth; more bits just add finer marks, so quieter sounds can be captured before they're lost in hiss (the dynamic range). It doesn't make anything louder, and it has nothing to do with pitch.
How quiet can it go? · loudest at the top, hiss at the bottom

3
Bitrate is the size of the backpack
How much data per second a file is allowed to use. For MP3 and other lossy files, the encoder decides what to leave behind to fit.
